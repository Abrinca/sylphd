Privacy: your sequences never leave your computer. Assemblies and reads are sketched in your browser and only the sketch (k-mer hashes and counts, no sequence) is sent to the server, which does not store or log it. In per-contig mode that is one small sketch per record, plus its length, GC and k-mer count. Only sizes, timings and request outcomes are recorded.

Terms: free for academic use via this web page and, for regular light use, via the API from your own scripts. For heavy or commercial use, please contact us first: https://abrinca.com/contact. See the terms of use.

What would you like to do?

Identify an assembly

Find the closest GTDB genomes of a bacterial or archaeal genome and get a species call.

  • Paste FASTA or upload a file (gzip is fine, up to 2 GiB), or a .sylsp sketch you made earlier
  • Species call at 95 % ANI, with the lineage relation of every hit
  • Sketched locally in your browser: the sequence never leaves your computer, only a small sketch is uploaded

Profile reads

Find out which bacteria and archaea are in a sequencing run, with their abundances.

  • Drop FASTQ or FASTA reads (.gz is fine): short, long or paired; or upload a .sylsp sketch you made earlier
  • Sketched locally in your browser: reads never leave your computer, only a sketch of a few MB is uploaded
  • Abundance by species up to phylum, GTDB, NCBI or all taxonomy sources, downloads

Not available: this browser cannot run the sketcher.

What does "same species" mean? A 95 % ANI cutoff is a convention, not a law: ANI values differ between methods, species can be millions of years old and genomically diverse, and some species fall on either side of the line.

Consensus options

A species joins the consensus when its divergence is within R times the best hit’s plus Z standard deviations of the ANI sampling noise. The defaults (1.5 and 2) resolve well-covered contigs to species and widen to genus or family only where the evidence is ambiguous.
